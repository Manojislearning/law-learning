# Statutory accuracy release 46 — Motor Vehicles Act and 2026 amendments

**Checked:** 11 October 2026  
**App:** KSLU Law Learning, first-year 3-year LL.B., Law of Torts Unit V.  
**Scope:** Nine primary-source-linked study cards for selected motor-accident compensation, insurance, Claims Tribunal provisions and 2022–2026 changes. **Not** a certification of the complete Motor Vehicles Act, Karnataka implementation, court interpretation, or the university curriculum applicable to the learner's admission cohort.

## Sources and temporal context

- India Code [Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, consolidated PDF](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1798/1/a1988-59.pdf) and [section index](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/18644?sam_handle=&view_type=browse). The base consolidated edition may not incorporate all recent changes.
- Ministry of Road Transport & Highways, [2022 hit-and-run compensation notification briefing](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=1801656&lang=2&reg=48). The compensation scheme applies from **1 April 2022**.
- MoRTH / PIB, [cashless road-accident treatment scheme report, 29 January 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2220125&lang=1&reg=6). It documents the scheme notified **5 May 2025**, under section 162.
- Gazette of India, [Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2026, Act 8 of 2026](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/271642.pdf), assent on 7 April and publication on 8 April 2026. The Motor Vehicles changes are in Schedule entry 51, including amendments to Sections 157, 161, 163, 164 and 166. Also listed at the official [Ministry of Coal Acts and Rules page](https://www.coal.gov.in/acts-rules-policies), which hosts the 2026 Act.
- Gazette of India, [notification S.O. 4100(E), 24 July 2026](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/274798.pdf), specifying **15 August 2026** commencement for schedule entry 51 concerning the Motor Vehicles Act. These official Gazette servers can occasionally be slow or unavailable.
- Detailed independent published descriptions of the amendment and notification were cross-checked, but **source-linked educational cards are not a substitute for reading the actual operative statutory text and latest notifications**.

## Important legal distinctions now taught

| Provision or scheme | What the app teaches | Important limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Section 164 | ₹5 lakh death, ₹2.5 lakh grievous hurt; no proof of fault required for the statutory route | Do not mistake this for Section 161 hit-and-run relief |
| Section 161 and 2022 scheme | Hit-and-run death ₹2 lakh and grievous hurt ₹50,000 | Qualifying hit-and-run means the vehicle cannot be identified despite reasonable efforts; **not** simply any driver fleeing |
| Section 162 and 2025 scheme | Cashless road accident medical treatment cover up to ₹1.5 lakh and up to 7 days | Hospital treatment mechanism, not identical to Claims Tribunal compensation |
| Sections 146–147 | Third-party insurance necessity and relevant policy conditions | Own-damage cover is not automatically a substitute for third-party insurance |
| Sections 149–150 | Insurer settlement procedure versus the duty to satisfy qualifying third-party awards | Beware old unamended section tables; defences and legal conditions apply |
| Sections 165–166 | Motor Accidents Claims Tribunals and applications for compensation | The Claims Tribunal is not determining criminal guilt |
| Section 166(3), **2026 amendment** | Initial six-month filing period, with a statutory proviso for a **further period not exceeding 12 months**, subject to sufficient cause and tribunal assessment | An application outside six months is not automatically barred under the amended proviso, but delay is not automatically condoned |
| Sections 163(3), 164(4), **2026 amendment** | Qualifying **ex-gratia relief** is treated differently from overlapping statutory compensation; the new provision protects against deduction under Section 164 | Do not extrapolate the exemption to all payment/award types |
| Section 157(2), **2026 amendment** | A vehicle transferee's application period for the insurer's certificate/policy change became **30 days**, replacing the former 14 days | The full transfer process must still satisfy the Act and applicable rules |

## UI and learning design

- **Current Law Check** now has three topics: BNS, Consumer Protection and **Motor Vehicles** (24 cards in total).
- The relevant Law of Torts Unit V page provides separate Consumer and Motor Vehicles routes, while the historical 1986 Consumer syllabus and current law remain distinguished.
- Each new card has an original factual example, a common misconception, shuffled choices, correction feedback and official sources.
- The new cards do **not** inflate points, streaks or mastery status and do not add persistent user-storage keys or change existing backup schema.
- Three-column tabs at wider widths reflow for narrower phones; the accessibility suite tests real rendered controls in Chromium at 320, 390 and 768 CSS pixels, and original IRAC/backup/lesson regression tests remain enabled.
