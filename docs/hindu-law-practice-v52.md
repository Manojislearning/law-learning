# Version 52 — Family Law–I (Hindu Law) Issue-Spotting Audit

**Date:** 11 October 2026  
**Scope:** Original first-year exam practice for all five imported KSLU Hindu Law units.  
**Evidence status:** Selected statutory texts checked; model IRAC answers are educational explanations, *not* court-certified ratios, KSLU marking schemes or full legal advice.

## Why this release

The previous release supported 60 IRAC cases across Contract–I, Torts, BNS and Constitutional Law–I. Family Law–I remained the missing substantive first-year subject. A separate quiz interface would increase user-interface complexity without improving transfer. The existing written issue-spotting and changed-facts mechanisms were extended instead.

## Four principal authoritative Acts

1. [Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 — India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1560/1/A1955-25Eng.pdf). Relevant Sections 2, 4, 5, 7, 11, 13B, 17, 18.
2. [Hindu Succession Act, 1956 — India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1713/1/AAA1956suc___30.pdf). Relevant Sections 4, 6 (2005 amendment), 8, 10, 14, 15 and the Class I Schedule.
3. [Hindu Adoptions and Maintenance Act, 1956 — India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1638/1/AA1956____78.pdf). Relevant Sections 6, 7 and 20.
4. [Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act, 1956 — India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1649/1/195632.pdf). Relevant Sections 8 and 13.

Each original case includes facts, a question, an IRAC reference explanation, a misconception to avoid and a direct official statutory link. The applicable subject/section is concealed in the main exam practice until the student types an answer. Four self-reported criteria prompt case-specific feedback; the app does not pretend to score essay correctness.

Each altered-facts exercise draws from a real original case and keeps its official sources. It asks the learner what changed legally rather than simply repeating the reference answer.

## Substantive pitfalls explicitly avoided

- A marriage violating the Hindu Marriage Act's **age condition** is not thereby within every ground of **Section 11** voidness. Separate statutory remedies or sanctions may still apply.
- A **custom** has no automatic immunity from codified law. The Act's stated exceptions and evidentiary requirements must be tested.
- Historic family-law schools are not substitutes for **post-2005 statutory succession**, including daughters' rights by birth and protections for certain older transactions.
- **HUF membership** does not automatically make every asset Mitakshara coparcenary property. Source of acquisition and other legally relevant evidence matter.
- Statutory restrictions on **pious obligation for family debt** must be evaluated with savings and other bases of liability.
- Section 14's female absolute ownership and its restricted-grant exception in subsection (2) must both be examined.
- A Hindu woman's **source of inherited property** may matter under Section 15(2); never calculate shares without classifying the estate.
- A guardian's transfer in breach of Hindu Minority and Guardianship Act Section 8 is described as **voidable**, not reflexively void.
- Adoption eligibility and consent depend on statutory prerequisites, not simply an informal document or ceremonial expression of intent.

## Verification and privacy

The release brings the main practice bank to **75 cases across five first-semester law subjects**, with **25 changed-facts exercises**. The existing selected-unit rotation, source-link validation, skipped-case treatment and in-memory weakness queue are preserved. Written essays are not uploaded or saved. No existing word scores, spaced-recall scheduling, profile data or backup schema were changed.

Automated checks cover the new 15-case, five-unit data bank; statutory sections and exception language; five parent-linked changed facts; disabled/hidden reference answers; syllabus routing; source links; session teardown and Chromium mobile/keyboard behaviour at 320, 390 and 768 CSS pixels.

**Limits:** A statute link is not a complete judicial-precedent check. Court interpretation, applicable custom, validity of earlier transactions, adoption conditions, property facts and subsequent amendments may change outcomes. These original cases are not actual KSLU question papers or a guarantee of marks.
