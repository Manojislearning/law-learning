## Fifteen narrowly audited study points

**Criminal Law–I: BNS (8)**

1. BNS 106(2) expressly excluded from the **2024** commencement notification; later notifications must be separately examined. Section 106(1) is not identical to 106(2).
2. Section 4 lists community service as one type of punishment, but individual offences and sentence requirements matter.
3. Section 61 criminal conspiracy compared with Section 62 general attempt punishment.
4. Section 80 dowry death versus Sections 85–86 cruelty; do not carry IPC 304B / 498A numbers into a BNS answer unchanged.
5. Sections 100, 101 (definitions), 103, 105 (punishments) and 106 negligent death (with commencement caveat). Section 103 **does not define murder**.
6. Sections 111, 112, 113 identify different types of organised crime and terrorist acts; each has its own elements.
7. Sections 303 theft and 304 snatching. Distinguish specific conduct rather than swapping labels.
8. Sections 316 criminal breach of trust, 317 stolen property, and 318 cheating. Separate entrusted property from deception and ordinary contractual default.

**Torts Unit V — Consumer Protection (7)**

1. The historical KSLU 2018–19 syllabus cites the Consumer Protection Act **1986**; the newer 2019 law is used for current-law study.
2. Section 2(7) includes qualifying online transactions and contains statutory exclusions.
3. Sections 2(10) defect (goods) and 2(11) deficiency (services).
4. Commission pecuniary jurisdiction under **2021 Rules**: District up to ₹50 lakh, State above ₹50 lakh and up to ₹2 crore, National above ₹2 crore. This is based on value **paid as consideration**, not simply compensation claimed. Check later rule changes and other jurisdiction requirements.
5. Section 69 standard two-year filing limitation, subject to recorded condonation for sufficient cause.
6. Section 39 District Commission findings/orders; relief is not automatically fixed at a single amount.
7. Section 10 CCPA versus distinct consumer dispute commissions.

The review is deliberately limited. It does not establish that every section of the two Acts, the motor-vehicle-law topics, or all KSLU examination material is legally up to date.

## Design and learning audit

- A home **Current Law Check** entry and contextual links from BNS and Torts Unit V make critical corrections visible before students memorise outdated numbers. It is a focused alternative to adding another sprawling study dashboard.
- Each point pairs an excerpt-length original explanation with an everyday scenario, an explicit misconception and a short three-choice check with feedback and the official source link.
- Choices change order to prevent memorising a single button position. No score, streak or misleading **mastered** badge is attached to a single correct response.
- All 30 KSLU unit outlines continue to retain **needs-review** cohort applicability. Six relevant units have an additional `lawAudit: selected-provisions-checked` marker in the ledger, not a blanket approval.
- Cross-regression tests guard the official source whitelist, law mappings, data integrity, deep links, correct feedback, offline cache, and existing save/backup mechanisms.
